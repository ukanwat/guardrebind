\begin{abstract}
Content that imitates an LLM safety guard's prompt format can make the guard grade a forged, harmless \emph{decoy} document instead of the real content, which is approved unchanged; we call this \emph{target rebinding}.
It is possible because most guards receive content by string interpolation into a plain-text prompt that also states the grading task.
Appending a forged query--document pair with a benign decoy to a flagged harmful request clears Shieldstral-1.0-3B on all 75 pre-registered held-out AdvBench requests, and the harmful text stays in the approved message. % src: results/confirm_log.txt
Format-adapted versions clear 30/30 flagged AdvBench requests on gpt-oss-safeguard-20B, which puts the policy behind a role boundary, and 29/29 on Llama Guard~4, which uses a fixed taxonomy. % src: results/confirm_crossmodel.csv (first 30 AdvBench rows 200-229)
Our benchmark, \guardrebind{}, runs 50 grammar-generated attack families against five guards; on every guard the strongest family clears $93$--$100\%$ of the items the guard flags with no attack. % src: results/bench/v2/bypass.csv, paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md
Training the guard to score content at least as high as its most harmful part cuts bypass on attack families and decoys held out of training from \tbd{r2: base heldout-family x heldout-decoy bypass}\% to \tbd{r2: T heldout x heldout bypass}\%, and the share of items cleared by a score-guided search attacker from \tbd{r2: base search}\% to \tbd{r2: T search}\%, with detection AUROC \tbd{r2: AUROC change}.
Counting every attack, the per-item worst case against the trained guard is \tbd{r2: worst case over all attacks incl.\ GCG}\%: a white-box gradient attack (GCG) still clears it, as it clears the undefended guard with no forged pair at all.
We release the benchmark, harness, adapters and every raw score.
\end{abstract}
