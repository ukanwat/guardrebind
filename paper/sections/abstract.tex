\begin{abstract}
Content that imitates an LLM safety guard's prompt format can make the guard grade a forged, harmless \emph{decoy} document instead of the real content, which is approved unchanged; we call this \emph{target rebinding}.
It works because most guards receive content by string interpolation into their grading prompt.
Appending a forged query--document pair clears Shieldstral-1.0-3B on all 75 pre-registered held-out AdvBench requests it flags, and format-adapted versions clear 30/30 on gpt-oss-safeguard-20B and 29/29 on Llama Guard~4. % src: results/confirm_log.txt; results/confirm_crossmodel.csv (first 30 AdvBench rows 200-229)
Our benchmark, \guardrebind{}, runs 52 attack families and 5 controls against five guards; on every guard the strongest family clears $93$--$100\%$ of flagged items. % src: results/bench/v2/bypass.csv, paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md
Training the guard to score content at least as high as its most harmful part cuts bypass on appended attack families, decoys and queries held out of training from $75\%$ to $0.8$--$1.6\%$; detection AUROC moves by under $0.01$, while flags on benign content carrying a forged pair rise from $0.2\%$ to $0.7$--$0.8\%$. % src: results/fix/r2_test_{base,t_seed0,t_seed1}.json
An adaptive attacker with score access still breaks it: a score-guided search clears the trained guard on $94\%$ of items ($68\%$ with trained boundary tokens), and the per-item worst case including GCG is $98\%$. % src: results/fix/r2_adaptive_*.json
More than half of these wins need no forged structure: benign user prompts placed before the request alone clear the trained guard on $52\%$ of items.
\end{abstract}
