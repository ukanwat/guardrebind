\begin{abstract}
We make LLM safety guards grade a harmless \emph{decoy} instead of the content they review, so harmful requests pass unchanged.
Most guards paste content into a plain-text prompt, and content that imitates it opens a second grading task (\emph{target rebinding}).
An appended forged query--document pair clears Shieldstral-1.0-3B on all 75 pre-registered held-out AdvBench requests it flags, adapted versions clear 30/30 on gpt-oss-safeguard-20B and 29/29 on Llama Guard~4, and on our \guardrebind{} benchmark the best of 52 attack families clears $93$--$100\%$ of flagged items on five guards. % src: results/confirm_log.txt; results/confirm_crossmodel.csv (first 30 AdvBench rows 200-229); results/bench/v2/bypass.csv, paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md
Training the guard to score content no lower than its most harmful part cuts the bypass rate on appended attack families, decoys and queries held out of training from $75\%$ to $0.8$--$1.6\%$, while AUROC moves under $0.01$ and flags on benign content with a forged pair rise from $0.2\%$ to $0.7\%$. % src: results/fix/r2_test_{base,t_seed0,t_seed1}.json
Adaptive attacks win: score-guided search clears the trained guard on $94\%$ of items ($98\%$ per-item worst case with GCG), and benign user prompts placed before the request, without forged structure, clear $52\%$. % src: results/fix/r2_adaptive_*.json
\end{abstract}
