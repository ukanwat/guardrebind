\begin{abstract}
Safety guards check content before an LLM application acts on it, and most paste that content into a plain-text prompt.
Content that imitates the prompt can append a second task, and the guard answers that one instead.
We call this \emph{target rebinding}. Our explanation is that the guard grades the last complete task in its input, here a harmless \emph{decoy}, so the harmful request goes through unchanged.
In a pre-registered test, an appended forged query and decoy clear all 75 held-out AdvBench requests that Shieldstral-1.0-3B flags. % src: results/confirm_log.txt [ablation] query_doc 75/75
The guard is not persuaded: a directive to ignore the text above clears none of them, and in a follow-up run a harmful decoy stops the attack. % src: results/confirm_log.txt [ablation] sentence_bare/authority_note 0/75; results/groundtruth_log.txt harmful decoy 0/39
On our \guardrebind{} benchmark, each of five guards has an attack family, chosen per guard, that clears at least $93\%$ of its flagged items. % src: results/bench/v2/bypass.csv (best family per guard, min 93.4 on Qwen3Guard); paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md
Rebinding explains this best on Shieldstral and gpt-oss-safeguard; on ShieldGemma and Llama Guard~3, generic evasion and trailing text respectively do part of the work, and Qwen3Guard falls mainly to a forgery of its whole task.
Training the guard to score content no lower than its most harmful part cuts attack success from $75\%$ to $0.8$--$1.6\%$ on attack families, decoys and forged queries held out of training. % src: results/fix/r2_test_{base,t_seed0,t_seed1}.json by_decoy_split.heldout.heldout.bypass_benign_decoy
All of these attacks append the forged structure after the request, and we added the held-out-decoy evaluation after a first round of test runs. % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md D4
Clean AUROC falls by at most $0.01$, but our test of non-inferiority is inconclusive. % src: auroc 0.810 vs 0.800 (B+T), 0.802-0.803 (T)
A score-guided search still beats this trained guard on 47 of 50 items, usually by placing forged structure before the request. % src: tab:adaptive T score-guided search 47/50 (r2_adaptive_t_seed0_search.json); paper/data/search_no_feedback.md (34 of 47 wins prepend)
On about half of the items where the search wins, the same search also finds benign text, with no forged structure, that clears the guard. % src: paper/data/search_no_feedback.md (26 plain-text-control wins of 47, all 26 also search wins)
\end{abstract}
