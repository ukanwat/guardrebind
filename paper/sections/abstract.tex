\begin{abstract}
Most LLM safety guards receive the content they judge by string interpolation into a plain-text grading prompt.
We show that content imitating that prompt can make the guard grade a harmless \emph{decoy} document in place of the real content, which is then approved unchanged.
We call this \emph{target rebinding}.
Appending a forged query--document pair clears Shieldstral-1.0-3B on all 75 pre-registered held-out AdvBench requests it flags, and format-adapted versions clear 30/30 on gpt-oss-safeguard-20B and 29/29 on Llama Guard~4. % src: results/confirm_log.txt; results/confirm_crossmodel.csv (first 30 AdvBench rows 200-229)
In our benchmark, \guardrebind{}, the strongest of 52 attack families clears $93$--$100\%$ of flagged items on each of five guards. % src: results/bench/v2/bypass.csv, paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md
Training the guard to score content at least as high as its most harmful part cuts bypass on appended attack families, decoys and queries held out of training from $75\%$ to $0.8$--$1.6\%$. % src: results/fix/r2_test_{base,t_seed0,t_seed1}.json
Detection AUROC moves by under $0.01$, and flags on benign content carrying a forged pair rise from $0.2\%$ to $0.7\%$.
An adaptive attacker still breaks it. A score-guided search clears the trained guard on $94\%$ of items, and the per-item worst case including the GCG gradient attack is $98\%$. % src: results/fix/r2_adaptive_*.json
Benign user prompts placed before the request, with no forged structure, already clear it on $52\%$ of items.
\end{abstract}
