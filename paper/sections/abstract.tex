\begin{abstract}
Safety guards check content against a policy before an LLM application acts on it.
Most guards paste content into a plain-text prompt, so content that imitates the prompt can open a second grading task, which the guard grades instead.
We call this \emph{target rebinding}: the guard grades the last complete task in its input, here a harmless \emph{decoy}, and the harmful request passes unchanged.
In a pre-registered test, an appended forged query and decoy let all 75 held-out AdvBench requests that Shieldstral-1.0-3B flags pass; on our \guardrebind{} benchmark, some attack family passes at least $93\%$ of flagged items on all five guards. % src: results/confirm_log.txt [ablation] query_doc 75/75; results/bench/v2/bypass.csv (best family per guard, min 93.4 on Qwen3Guard)
Training the guard to score content no lower than its most harmful part cuts bypass from $75\%$ to $0.8$--$1.6\%$ on attack families, decoys and forged queries that were held out of training (all appended after the content), with AUROC within $0.01$. % src: results/fix/r2_test_{base,t_seed0,t_seed1}.json by_decoy_split.heldout.heldout.bypass_benign_decoy; auroc 0.810 vs 0.802-0.803
This closes the appended channel, but adaptive score-guided search still gets past the trained guard on $94\%$ of items, mostly by placing forged structure before the request. % src: results/fix/r2_adaptive_t_seed0_search.json worst_case 47/50; paper/data/search_no_feedback.md (34 of 47 wins prepend)
\end{abstract}
