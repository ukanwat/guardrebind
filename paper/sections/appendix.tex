\section{Protocol and Pre-registered Outcomes}
\label{app:protocol}

We froze the first protocol before the confirmatory run and did not edit it after seeing results, and we record every deviation from either protocol. % src: PROTOCOL.md, PROTOCOL_v2.md
These runs are exploratory and fall outside both frozen protocols: the wider set of 100 requests, the three-decoy comparison, the off-topic-query control, the forged-query (attacker knowledge) variants, the forged-verdict and split-payload controls, the real-control-token probe, the colon-label detector and label-normalisation tests, the clean-copy ablation and the GCG control without a forged pair.
The second protocol was frozen before any test-split run and has four recorded deviations. % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md (Deviations D1-D4)
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item D1 adds the variability design: three instantiations per item and family, two paraphrased deployment policies on six families, and three training seeds per defense condition.
  \item D2 replaces Qwen3Guard's conversation-only native forgery with the full-task forgery found on the train split, and adds the length control.
  \item D3 trims compute: 5{,}000 training items with two attacks each, three seeds for B$+$T and two for T and naive adversarial training, GCG on 20 items at 250 steps, and structural search on 50 items.
  \item D4 was recorded after a first round of test-split defense runs and before the reported ones, so it is a deviation made with test results in view. The fixed-decoy conditions of \cref{tab:fix-fixed} come from that first round. D4 draws training decoys and forged queries from one half of each pool and evaluates on the other half, adds the score-guided search and the two training-free baselines, and replaces the D3 seed plan with a smaller set to save compute: two seeds for T and one for B$+$T and the clean-copy target. % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md D4 ("lean defense set ... replaces the D3 seed plan")
\end{itemize}

\section{Benchmark Grammar}
\label{app:grammar}

The attack grammar has the axes below. The full grammar yields 659 forms (654 attacks, 5 controls), and the core panel has 63. % src: src/bench/attacks_grammar.py grammar(core=False) 659, grammar(core=True) 63, CONTROLS (5)
A family is held out if its syntax is JSON, YAML, markdown bold or control token, if its language is neither English nor Spanish, or if it has at least three forged pairs. Spanish-label families are therefore held out only through the syntax or pair-count rule. The defense is trained on the remaining families. % src: src/bench/attacks_grammar.py is_heldout
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item label syntax: guard-native tags, angle brackets, square brackets, markdown, plain colon, abbreviations, JSON, XML, YAML, control tokens;
  \item number of forged pairs: 1--5; position: append, prepend, middle;
  \item decoy harm: benign, borderline, harmful; forged query: exact, paraphrase, generic, out-of-domain; language; encoding: plain text or real control tokens.
\end{itemize}
Per-family, per-category and per-source bypass and threshold sensitivity are released with the artifacts.

\section{Additional Attack Analyses}
\label{app:extra}

\paragraph{Activation patching.}
Activation patching on Shieldstral is consistent with rebinding but does not identify the circuit that implements it.
We run the attacked input and the same input with request and decoy swapped ($n=100$), using for each request a decoy of the closest token length and matching positions by role, not by index, which keeps any remaining length mismatch from misaligning them.
Patching the residual stream at the final position flips the verdict on at least $99\%$ of inputs at every layer from 20 onward (of 26, numbered from 0). % src: src/mech/patching.py (match_decoy, role-matched position sets); patching_test_n100.json n_layers 26, final.into_attacked.flip_rate[20..25] = 0.99-1.0
Patching the forged labels, the forged query or the decoy's positions at any layer moves the yes/no logit margin by at most $0.05$ of the gap between the two inputs and flips at most $3\%$ of verdicts. % src: patching_test_n100.json summary.final.into_attacked.flip_rate[20]=0.99; forged_* peak_effect <= 0.043, max flip_rate 0.03

\paragraph{Benchmark variations.}
Real control-token syntax changes bypass relative to angle-bracket labels by $-1.5$ to $+10.4$ points, which only partly supports our prediction that it would raise bypass. % src: bypass.csv control_token vs angle, query_doc and full_triple: shieldstral +10.4/+6.3, llama +1.6/+0.5, shieldgemma +6.1/+4.6, qwen -1.5/+1.3
Across the three deployment policies, bypass moves by at most $6.8$ points on Shieldstral, Llama Guard~3 and Qwen3Guard, and by up to $13.7$ points on ShieldGemma. % src: policy_range.csv

\paragraph{Multiple verdicts from gpt-oss-safeguard.}
On stacked forged pairs gpt-oss-safeguard sometimes returns one verdict per content block.
We count an item as flagged if any verdict is 1, the reading least favourable to the attack, chosen after the multi-verdict replies were seen. % src: results/bench/v2/gpt_oss_summary.md (Raw outputs: '1\n0' x254, '1\n0\n0' x143)
Reading only the last verdict, as a pipeline acting on the trailing label would, raises two stacked-pair families from $2.9$--$3.5\%$ to $87.3$--$90.6\%$ and moves every other family by under one point. % src: paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md, mean column, parse = strict vs last (angle query_doc n=2 q=generic 2.9 -> 90.6; md_bold query_doc n=3 3.5 -> 87.3; all others < 1 point)


\section{Defense Implementation and Training}
\label{app:defense-impl}

\paragraph{Bounded format.}
The bounded format renders the stock chat template with a placeholder document and splits it into a trusted prefix and suffix. The content is encoded on its own with special-token parsing disabled, and the two reserved boundary tokens (unused in pretraining) are inserted around it. % src: src/fix/inputs.py
The template puts a space before the document, which the tokenizer would otherwise merge into the content's first token. We move that space into the trusted prefix, so plain documents tokenize exactly as they do in the stock format (verified on 1{,}600 documents: AdvBench rows 200--299 and the clean test sample). % src: src/fix/inputs.py (_content); scripts/check_bounded_tokenization.py -> results/fix/bounded_tokenization_check.json (1600/1600 identical)

\paragraph{Training.}
We train a LoRA (rank 16, $\alpha=32$) on the language model's attention and MLP projections with AdamW at learning rate $10^{-4}$, batch 8, one epoch and maximum length 384. The loss is binary cross-entropy between the yes/no logit margin and the segment-max teacher target. LoRA does not touch the embeddings. In B$+$T a trainable two-row offset, initialised at zero, is added to the embeddings of the two boundary tokens (ids 36 and 37), and every other embedding stays frozen. % src: src/fix/distill.py (boundary_delta) % src: src/fix/distill.py, PROTOCOL_v2.md (Fixed training recipe)
The data are 5{,}000 train-split prompt items, each clean and with two training-family attacks, plus 1{,}250 structured-benign items, for 16{,}250 examples. One epoch is 2{,}032 steps, the last batch partial. Each attacked example's decoy and forged query are a seeded draw from the training halves of the pools: 20 of the 40 benign decoys, 3 of 6 borderline and 4 of 8 harmful decoys, and 4 of the 8 forged queries at each relatedness level. The hard negatives are train-split structured-benign items. % src: src/fix/distill.py (build); logs/pod/build_r2.log (wrote 16250 examples); logs/pod/train_r2_t_seed0.log (2032 steps)
Naive adversarial training and the clean-copy ablation use the same examples with different targets.

\paragraph{Compute and adaptive budgets.}
Training and the test-split defense, adaptive and mechanism runs used one NVIDIA RTX 4090. A training run takes about 17 minutes (1{,}028\,s for T and 1{,}037\,s for B$+$T at the last logged step, 2{,}030 of 2{,}032). The benchmark ran on an Apple-silicon laptop GPU. % src: logs/pod/train_r2_t_seed0.log, logs/pod/train_r2_bt_seed0.log (last line before 'saved': step 2030, s 1028 / 1037)
The score-guided search has 300 guard calls per item on 50 items, starting from 20 random candidates and then mutating batches of 10 children with restart probability 0.2. The structural search scores all 684 attacks on each of 50 items (batch 128). GCG optimises a 20-token suffix inside the forged document for 250 steps (batch 32 candidates per step) on 20 items, and stops on an item at its first success. % src: src/fix/adaptive.py; scripts/queue_round2.sh

\paragraph{Content tokens.}
Disabling special-token parsing leaves atomic any added token that the tokenizer does not mark special~\citep{zhan2026samebytes,yang2026nameless}, so we checked which added-vocabulary ids the content encoder can still emit.
For each guard tokenizer we encoded every added token's string on its own, embedded in plain text and in 500 random concatenations, with special-token parsing disabled, and recorded any added or special id in the output. % src: scripts/check_content_tokens.py, results/fix/content_token_check.json
No added or special id is reachable from content on Shieldstral, the guard the defense is trained on (1000 added tokens, all special, with the boundary tokens at ids 36 and 37), or on Llama Guard~3 1B (256, all special).
On Qwen3Guard-0.6B, 12 of 26 added tokens are not marked special and stay atomic, among them the tool-call tags \texttt{<tool\_call>} and \texttt{</tool\_call>} and the fill-in-the-middle markers. On ShieldGemma 2B, 242 of 249 are not special (the \texttt{<unused$k$>} and other reserved markers) and also stay atomic.
Content can produce these ids, so a boundary-token design on these tokenizers must not use them as boundary tokens.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

The runs used these Hugging Face revisions: Shieldstral-1.0-3B \texttt{003ec7e}, Llama Guard~3 1B \texttt{acf7aaf}, Qwen3Guard-Gen-0.6B \texttt{fada3b2} and ShieldGemma 2B \texttt{d1dffc9}. Local guards are scored from a single forward pass with no sampling. The score is the probability of the unsafe label renormalised over the guard's label tokens, computed in fp32 from bf16 hidden states. We reached the API-served guards, gpt-oss-safeguard-20B and Llama Guard~4 12B, through OpenRouter at temperature 0 without pinning a provider. Every benchmark call to gpt-oss-safeguard was served by Groq, as recorded in the response metadata; the cross-model runs did not record the provider. % src: src/crossmodel.py, src/gpt_oss_mechanism.py, src/bench/api_bench.py (no provider field in the request; response 'provider' stored); results/bench/v2/gpt_oss_safeguard_20b.jsonl provider = Groq on 19454/19454 rows; results/confirm_crossmodel.csv has no provider column; revisions = local HF cache refs/main, equal to the Hub main sha on 2026-10-17 (unchanged since before the runs); the code loads main and does not pin revisions
Dataset sources and licenses: AdvBench, HarmBench, Aegis~2.0, BeaverTails, the OpenAI moderation evaluation and ToxicChat, with XSTest and OR-Bench as over-refusal sources. We use the non-commercial sets (ToxicChat, BeaverTails) for research only. % src: src/bench/data.py load_all() sources
All hyperparameters, splits and the full protocol are in \cref{app:protocol,app:defense-impl}.
