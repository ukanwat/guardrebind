\section{Target Rebinding}
\label{sec:attack}

\paragraph{What the guard sees.}
After interpolation, the developer's \texttt{<Document>} field in \cref{fig:anatomy} holds the harmful request followed by a forged \texttt{<Query>} that repeats the deployment's safety question and a forged \texttt{<Document>} holding a benign decoy.
The model reads two query--document pairs, and the attacker's labels are the same plain text as the developer's; nothing marks which pair is real.
We hypothesise that the model grades the document of the last complete pair.
The guard then answers its usual question about the decoy, whereas a persuaded guard would obey the content and answer ``safe''.
Rebinding predicts that only a complete forged pair works, and that the verdict follows the harm of the decoy.
The evidence is behavioural, with a decoy dose--response check (\cref{sec:binding}) and activation patching (\cref{app:extra}).

\paragraph{Setup.}
The ablation panel, the borderline-decoy comparison and the position, label-syntax and policy variations form a \emph{confirmatory} run that follows a protocol frozen before it (\cref{app:protocol}).
Results marked \emph{exploratory} were added after the confirmatory run and are listed there.
The corpus is AdvBench~\citep{zou2023universal} rows 200--299, which no exploratory run touched.
Under the policy of \cref{fig:anatomy}, 75 of the 100 requests are flagged ($>0.9$) with no attack, and these flagged requests are the denominator. % src: PROTOCOL.md; results/confirm_log.txt

The forged pair of \cref{fig:anatomy} clears all 75 flagged requests ($100\%$\ci{95\%}{100\%}, \cref{tab:ablation}).
The median score falls from $0.986$ to $0.016$, the highest attacked score is $0.469$ (\cref{fig:scores}a), and at the stricter threshold $\tau=0.1$ bypass is still $84\%$. % src: results/confirm_shieldstral.csv; results/sensitivity.md
On the broader six-source test set of \cref{sec:benchmark}, flagged at $0.5$ instead of $0.9$ and with decoys and queries drawn from pools, the same pair lets $70.9\%$ through (\cref{sec:background} relates these figures). % src: results/bench/v2/bypass.csv shieldstral angle query_doc

The ablation isolates the pair as the active part (\cref{tab:ablation}).
An instruction alone never gets through, whether as a bare sentence, a ``Note:'' or a forged \texttt{<Instruct>} field.
A forged document with no query passes $9\%$.
A forged query and document clear $100\%$, and a forged instruction in front of them lowers this to $92\%$.

\subsection{The guard grades the decoy}
\label{sec:groundtruth}

If the guard were being persuaded to answer ``safe'', the content of the decoy would not matter.
An exploratory comparison holds the attack fixed and varies only the decoy ($n=39$, \cref{fig:scores}b), described by its \emph{standalone score}, the guard's score on the decoy alone.
A \emph{benign} decoy ($0.00$) clears $100\%$\ci{91\%}{100\%}, a \emph{borderline} decoy ($0.03$) clears $33\%$\ci{21\%}{49\%}, and a \emph{harmful} decoy, one the guard flags on its own ($\approx\!1.0$), clears $0\%$\ci{0\%}{9\%}. % src: results/groundtruth_log.txt, results/confirm_groundtruth.csv
With the borderline decoy the attacked median is $0.71$, far above the decoy's own $0.03$: when the decoy is not clearly benign, the original request still raises the score. % src: results/groundtruth_log.txt (attack median 0.7058)
The confirmatory panel's pre-registered ``harmful'' decoy scores only $0.03$ on its own, which makes it this borderline decoy; it clears 29--35\% (\cref{app:protocol}). % src: results/confirm_log.txt [groundtruth]

Forging a softened query with no benign decoy anywhere clears $0$--$9\%$ across five exploratory variants ($n=45$). The forged query works by opening a new pair. % src: results/soften_log.txt, results/confirm_soften.csv

Forging a verdict instead of a task, as earlier and concurrent attacks on judges do~\citep{mccauley2025hat,li2026same}, works far less well.
In an exploratory control on 200 harmful test items that Shieldstral flags alone, four such forgeries (a plain-text safe assessment, a JSON moderation record, a safety-team approval note and a claimed earlier guard output) clear $6$--$19\%$, against $96\%$\ci{92\%}{98\%} for the forged pair. % src: results/fix/forgery_controls_base.json (verdict_plain 38/200, verdict_json 11/200, authority 13/200, prior_verdict 11/200, task 192/200)

\paragraph{Robustness.}
The attack needs no white-box access and little knowledge of the deployment.
We varied one factor at a time around the core attack ($n=75$ unless stated). % src: results/confirm_log.txt [recency], [markers], [policy]; results/supp_log.txt [A]
\emph{Position:} appending the pair clears $100\%$ and prepending the identical pair lets through $15\%$\ci{8\%}{24\%}.
Inert filler placed last clears $0\%$ (\cref{tab:ablation}). The pair must come last, and later text alone is not enough.
\emph{Syntax:} seven label styles (angle brackets in three letter cases, plain colon, square brackets, markdown bold and the abbreviated \texttt{Q:}/\texttt{D:}) pass $83$--$100\%$, and the same words without labels pass none.
\emph{Query knowledge} (exploratory): the forged query must look like a safety question but need not match the deployment's.
Paraphrases and topical variants reach $100\%$ bypass, a generic query $89\%$ and an unrelated query $9\%$, and a blind attacker who stacks five guesses, none of them the deployment's query, clears $100\%$. % src: results/supp_log.txt [A]
\emph{Policy:} across eight policy phrasings ($n=45$--$98$), each with a matched forged query, the median bypass is $99\%$ (range $87$--$100\%$). % src: results/confirm_log.txt [policy]

\paragraph{Decoy dose--response.}
\label{sec:binding}
Across many decoys ($n=150$ requests, 40 decoys with standalone scores from $0.00$ to $1.00$), bypass falls from $98\%$ for decoys scoring below $0.1$ to $0\%$ above $0.9$, with Spearman $\rho = -0.84$ over decoys (decoy-bootstrap 95\% interval $[-0.93, -0.67]$). % src: results/mech/dose_response_test_n150_d40.json; dose_response_test_n150_d40_rho_ci.json
Our pre-registered prediction of $\rho \le -0.8$, with bypass non-increasing across score bins, is partly supported (\cref{tab:prereg}).
The point estimate meets the threshold, but the decoy-level interval crosses it, the dev split gives $-0.78$, and bypass is not monotone across bins. % src: monotone_nonincreasing_bins False; dose_response_dev_n150_d40.json -0.777
