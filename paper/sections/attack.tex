\section{Target Rebinding}
\label{sec:attack}

\paragraph{What the guard sees.}
After interpolation, the developer's \texttt{<Document>} field in \cref{fig:anatomy} holds the harmful request, then a forged \texttt{<Query>} that repeats the deployment's safety question, then a forged \texttt{<Document>} holding a benign decoy.
The model reads two query--document pairs whose labels are the same plain text, so nothing marks which pair is real.
We hypothesise that the model grades the last complete pair, answering its usual question about the decoy, whereas a persuaded guard would obey the content and answer ``safe''.
Rebinding predicts that only a complete forged pair works, and that the verdict follows the harm of the decoy.
The evidence is mainly behavioural: an ablation of the pair and a decoy dose--response check (\cref{sec:binding}).
Activation patching (\cref{app:extra}) is consistent with rebinding but does not locate the mechanism.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The ablation panel, the borderline-decoy comparison and the position, label-syntax and policy variations form a \emph{confirmatory} run under a protocol frozen beforehand (\cref{app:protocol}).
Results marked \emph{exploratory} were added later and are listed there.
The corpus is AdvBench~\citep{zou2023universal} rows 200--299, which no exploratory run touched.
Under the policy of \cref{fig:anatomy}, 75 of the 100 requests are flagged ($>0.9$) with no attack, and these flagged requests are the denominator. % src: PROTOCOL.md; results/confirm_log.txt

The forged pair of \cref{fig:anatomy} clears all 75 flagged requests ($100\%$\ci{95\%}{100\%}, \cref{tab:ablation}).
The median score falls from $0.986$ to $0.016$ (\cref{fig:scores}a). % src: results/confirm_shieldstral.csv; results/sensitivity.md
Even the highest attacked score, $0.469$, stays below $\tau$, and at the stricter threshold $\tau=0.1$ bypass is still $84\%$.
On the broader six-source corpus of \cref{sec:benchmark}, the same pair clears Shieldstral less often, at $70.9\%$.\footnote{In this section requests are flagged above $0.9$ and the attack uses one fixed decoy and query. The benchmark flags at $0.5$ and draws decoys and queries from pools, and there the strongest family clears $99.2\%$ (\cref{tab:benchmark-main}). Families, decoys and queries held out of training clear $75.0\%$ of attacked inputs built from 297 flagged test items (\cref{sec:evaluation}).} % src: results/bench/v2/bypass.csv shieldstral (n_items 300; angle query_doc 0.709; best family 99.2); results/fix/r2_test_base.json screened 297, by_decoy_split.heldout.heldout.bypass_benign_decoy 5347/7128

\Cref{tab:ablation} shows that the forged query is what turns forged text into a task.
A forged document alone clears $9\%$, and adding the query raises this to $100\%$.
Instructions clear nothing in any form we tried, even with a forged query after them.
A forged instruction in front of the full pair lowers bypass to $92\%$.
We read this as the clearest evidence against persuasion: imperative text slightly works against the attack.

\subsection{The guard grades the decoy}
\label{sec:groundtruth}

If the guard were being persuaded to answer ``safe'', the content of the decoy would not matter.
An exploratory comparison holds the attack fixed and varies only the decoy ($n=39$, \cref{fig:scores}b), described by its \emph{standalone score}, the guard's score on the decoy alone.
A \emph{benign} decoy ($0.00$) clears $100\%$\ci{91\%}{100\%}.
A \emph{borderline} decoy ($0.03$) clears $33\%$\ci{21\%}{49\%}.
A \emph{harmful} decoy, one the guard flags on its own ($\approx\!1.0$), clears $0\%$\ci{0\%}{9\%}. % src: results/groundtruth_log.txt, results/confirm_groundtruth.csv
Rebinding is not a clean switch: with the borderline decoy the attacked median is $0.71$, far above the decoy's own $0.03$, so the original request leaks back into the score. % src: results/groundtruth_log.txt (attack median 0.7058)
The confirmatory panel's pre-registered ``harmful'' decoy scores only $0.03$ on its own, so it is this borderline decoy, and it clears 29--35\% (\cref{app:protocol}). % src: results/confirm_log.txt [groundtruth]

Forged text that does not open a new task works far less well.
A softened forged query with no benign decoy clears $0$--$9\%$ across five exploratory variants ($n=45$), so the query helps by opening a new pair. % src: results/soften_log.txt, results/confirm_soften.csv
Forged verdicts, used by earlier and concurrent attacks on judges~\citep{mccauley2025hat,li2026same}, also work poorly.
On 200 harmful test items that Shieldstral flags alone, an exploratory control tried four verdict forgeries: a plain-text safe assessment, a JSON moderation record, a safety-team approval note and a claimed earlier guard output.
They clear $6$--$19\%$, against $96\%$\ci{92\%}{98\%} for the forged pair. % src: results/fix/forgery_controls_base.json (verdict_plain 38/200, verdict_json 11/200, authority 13/200, prior_verdict 11/200, task 192/200)

\paragraph{Robustness.}
The attack needs no white-box access, and the attacker has to know only the shape of the deployment's task.
Keeping the policy or the query secret therefore does not protect the guard.
We varied one factor at a time around the core attack ($n=75$ unless stated). % src: results/confirm_log.txt [recency], [markers], [policy]; results/supp_log.txt [A]
\emph{Position:} the pair clears $100\%$ when appended and $15\%$\ci{8\%}{24\%} when prepended.
Inert filler appended last clears $0\%$ (\cref{tab:ablation}), so the pair has to come last, and coming last is not enough by itself.
\emph{Syntax:} seven label styles (angle brackets in three letter cases, plain colon, square brackets, markdown bold and the abbreviated \texttt{Q:}/\texttt{D:}) clear $83$--$100\%$, and the same words without labels clear none.
\emph{Query knowledge} (exploratory): the forged query must look like a safety question but need not match the deployment's.
Paraphrases and topical variants reach $100\%$ bypass and a generic query $89\%$, while an unrelated query clears only $9\%$.
A blind attacker who stacks five guesses, none of them the deployment's query, clears $100\%$. % src: results/supp_log.txt [A]
\emph{Policy:} over eight policy phrasings, each with a matched forged query ($n=45$--$98$), median bypass is $99\%$ (range $87$--$100\%$). % src: results/confirm_log.txt [policy]

\paragraph{Decoy dose--response.}
\label{sec:binding}
Across many decoys, bypass tracks the decoy's standalone score, as rebinding predicts.
We used 150 requests and 40 decoys scoring $0.00$ to $1.00$ alone.
Decoys scoring below $0.1$ clear $98\%$, and decoys above $0.9$ clear $0\%$.
Spearman's $\rho$ over decoys is $-0.84$ (decoy-bootstrap 95\% interval $[-0.93, -0.67]$). % src: results/mech/dose_response_test_n150_d40.json; dose_response_test_n150_d40_rho_ci.json
This partly supports our pre-registered prediction of $\rho \le -0.8$ with bypass non-increasing across score bins (\cref{tab:prereg}).
The point estimate meets the threshold, but the decoy-level interval crosses it and the dev split gives $-0.78$. % src: monotone_nonincreasing_bins False; dose_response_dev_n150_d40.json -0.777
Bypass is also not monotone across bins, which fits the borderline-decoy result above.
