\section{Background and Threat Model}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{How guards receive content}

\paragraph{Shieldstral.}
Shieldstral-1.0-3B~\citep{calvi2026shieldstral} is a policy-adaptive guard trained to answer a yes/no question about a document.
Its model card specifies a fixed system prompt and a user message with three labelled fields (\cref{lst:shieldstral}). % src: src/prompt.py (verbatim from the model card)
The fields are plain text.
The Shieldstral paper describes no special-token delimiters and contains no adversarial-robustness or prompt-injection evaluation (related-work review of the paper's HTML).
We read the guard's score as $P(\text{yes})$ from the yes/no logits renormalised over the two answers, and say a document is \emph{flagged} if the score exceeds $\tau$ and \emph{cleared} otherwise.

\paragraph{Llama Guard.}
Llama Guard~\citep{inan2023llama} and its successors~\citep{grattafiori2024llama,chi2024llama,meta2025llamaguard4} grade a conversation against a fixed category taxonomy (S1--S14 for Llama Guard~4).
The provider's chat template wraps the conversation in \texttt{<BEGIN CONVERSATION>} and \texttt{<END CONVERSATION>} lines and the model emits \texttt{safe} or \texttt{unsafe} followed by a category.
There is no policy in the prompt for content to override; the delimiters are again plain text.
The Llama Guard~4 model card itself notes that the model ``may be susceptible to adversarial attacks or prompt injection attacks''~\citep{meta2025llamaguard4}.

\paragraph{gpt-oss-safeguard.}
gpt-oss-safeguard-20B and -120B~\citep{openai2025gptosssafeguard} reason over a developer-provided policy placed in the system (or developer) role and were post-trained to follow an instruction hierarchy~\citep{wallace2024instruction}.
The content is a separate user message.
This is the design one would reach for as a mitigation: the policy is behind a role boundary that the content cannot cross.
The vendor's report rates its prompt-injection robustness at 0.512 for the 20B model and describes injection robustness as less important for the intended internal use~\citep{openai2025gptosssafeguard}.

\subsection{Threat model}
\label{sec:threat}

\paragraph{Deployment.}
A developer places a guard $G$ in front of a downstream system.
The developer chooses the instruction, the grading question (or policy) and the format, and inserts untrusted content $c$ into the content slot.
If $G$ clears $c$, $c$ is forwarded unchanged.
We consider three channels by which $c$ arrives:
(i) a user message in a chat deployment;
(ii) a retrieved passage in retrieval-augmented generation; and
(iii) a tool result in an agent loop, where clearing the guard may cause an action to run with no further model in the loop.

\paragraph{Attacker.}
The attacker controls only the content $c$.
They do not control the instruction, the grading question, the system prompt, the decoding or the threshold, and they do not see scores.
We consider attackers with varying knowledge of the deployment's grading question, from exact knowledge to none (\cref{sec:query}).
For \cref{sec:adaptive} we additionally grant white-box access to the defended model's weights and the boundary-token mechanism.

\paragraph{Goal.}
The attacker wants a harmful payload $h$, which $G$ flags on its own, to be cleared while remaining present in the forwarded content.
An attack that removes, paraphrases or obfuscates $h$ is out of scope; the downstream model must still be able to read and act on $h$.
This distinguishes target rebinding from evasion attacks that hide harmful content from a classifier~\citep{hackett2025bypassing,mangaokar2024prp}.

\paragraph{Success criterion.}
A screened document is one that the honest deployment flags ($G(h) > 0.9$ for Shieldstral; majority verdict \texttt{unsafe}/\texttt{1} for the label-only guards). % src: PROTOCOL.md, Fixed design
An attack succeeds on $h$ if $G(h \oplus p) < \tau$, where $p$ is the attacker's appended payload and $\tau = 0.5$ unless stated. % src: PROTOCOL.md
Bypass rate is the fraction of screened documents on which the attack succeeds.
