\section{\texorpdfstring{\guardrebind{}}{GuardRebind}: the Attack across Five Guards}
\label{sec:benchmark}

Forged task structure clears every guard we tested, though not always by rebinding.
In our benchmark, \guardrebind{}, each of five guards has an attack family that clears $93.4$--$100\%$ of its flagged items (\cref{tab:benchmark-main}).

\subsection{Benchmark design}

\paragraph{Guards and data.}
We run four open-weight guards locally, Shieldstral-1.0-3B~\citep{calvi2026shieldstral}, Llama Guard~3 1B~\citep{grattafiori2024llama}, ShieldGemma 2B~\citep{zeng2024shieldgemma} and Qwen3Guard 0.6B~\citep{zhao2025qwenguard}, and gpt-oss-safeguard-20B through the OpenRouter API~\citep{openai2025gptosssafeguard}.
Each guard's score is its $P(\text{unsafe})$ renormalised over its own label tokens (for Qwen3Guard, $P(\text{Unsafe})+P(\text{Controversial})$, its strict mode). gpt-oss-safeguard is label-only, so its score is its verdict. Content goes wherever the guard's format puts the material under review.
Harmful and benign items come from AdvBench~\citep{zou2023universal}, HarmBench~\citep{mazeika2024harmbench}, Aegis~2.0~\citep{ghosh2025aegis}, BeaverTails~\citep{ji2023beavertails}, the OpenAI moderation evaluation~\citep{markov2022holistic} and ToxicChat~\citep{lin2023toxicchat}.
XSTest-safe~\citep{rottger2023xstest} and OR-Bench-hard~\citep{cui2024orbench} add benign items that guards tend to over-flag (\emph{over-refusal}).
This gives 20k harmful and 20k benign \emph{items}, split 60/10/30 by a hash of the normalised text. % src: src/bench/data.py load_all()
The core set is a stratified sample of 600 harmful and 600 benign test items per guard, cut to 200 + 200 for gpt-oss-safeguard, which sits behind a paid API. % src: results/bench/v2/gpt_oss_summary.md (core_set(n=200))

\paragraph{Attack grammar.}
Attacks come from a grammar whose axes are label syntax, number of forged pairs (1--5), position, decoy harm, forged-query relatedness, language, and encoding (real control tokens or plain text), with levels in \cref{app:grammar}.
Position is append (after the request), prepend (before it) or middle (benign filler, then the request, then the forged structure; it differs from append only by the leading filler).
An attack \emph{family} is the structural choice: one level on each axis. A \emph{form} adds one fixed decoy sentence and one fixed forged query, so a family can have several forms. For example, one family appends a single forged query and document in English angle-bracket labels, with a benign decoy and a generic forged query.
An \emph{instantiation} of a family draws the two sentences from pools instead: one of 40 benign decoys and one of eight generic safety questions.
% src: src/bench/attacks_grammar.py (Attack.family, instantiate, QUERY_POOLS_V1 generic, 8 per pool); _INST_DECOY_POOLS benign = 40
The core panel we run has 52 attack families and 5 controls, one of which is the no-attack condition (counts in \cref{app:grammar}). % src: src/bench/attacks_grammar.py len(grammar(core=True)) = 63 = 58 attack forms + 5 controls, 57 families = 52 attack + 5 control
The \emph{guard-native forgery} forges the guard's whole grading task, writing its turn or template markers as plain text (see \emph{What counts as a task} in \cref{sec:bench-results}). % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md (Deviation D2)
This forgery is long for Qwen3Guard (about 1.35k characters, against 110--210 for the other guards), so every guard also gets a length control, label-free benign filler of matched length. % src: len of the guard_native query_doc payload per GUARD_SPECS (attacks_grammar.py): qwen3guard 1349, others 113-205

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The full core attack panel runs on the core set for every guard under its default policy, and six families also run under two paraphrased deployment policies. % src: results/bench/v2/run_config.json
Each item--family cell draws three instantiations, seeded by the item and family. Bypass is their mean, with a 95\% interval from a two-level bootstrap over items and then instantiations, as pre-registered (\cref{app:protocol}). % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md (Deviation D1, K=3); PROTOCOL_v2.md D1(a)

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:bench-results}

The best family is chosen after the fact, so we also report the guard-native forgery, fixed before any test-split run (for Qwen3Guard, revised on the train split, D2 in \cref{app:protocol}), which clears $71.7$--$100\%$ on every guard.
Bypass counts only flagged items, so it measures how much of a guard's working detection the attack removes, not its clean accuracy.
Clean miss rates run from $10.7\%$ on Qwen3Guard to $72.0\%$ on ShieldGemma, whose $100\%$ best bypass covers only the minority of harmful items it flags.

Instructions alone do little: instruction-only payloads clear at most $7.0\%$ on four guards.
On ShieldGemma they clear $20.2\%$\ci{14.3\%}{26.8\%}, which breaks the $10\%$ bound we pre-registered for every guard.
The decoy's harm matters on the same four guards: with a harmful decoy, the strongest forgeries clear at most $13.6\%$.

The guard's format decides how cheap the attack is.
On Llama Guard~3 a single forged pair with a benign decoy clears $84$--$100\%$ in every syntax and language we tried, and $41$ of $52$ attack families reach $70\%$ or more. % src: bypass.csv llama_guard_3_1b default, query_doc n=1 decoy=benign families min 84.0 (colon lang=fr), 41/52 families >= 0.70
Qwen3Guard rebinds reliably only to a forgery of its full task, and generic labels stay at or below $18.3\%$.

Llama Guard~3 is partly swayed by any trailing text: benign text appended with no forged pair clears it $41.2\%$\ci{36.1\%}{46.0\%}, against $4.3\%$ on Shieldstral. Part of its bypass is therefore recency, although the forged pair still adds up to $59$ points. % src: bypass.csv control|benign_tail
Under the benchmark's looser flag rule, a forged document alone clears $25$--$37\%$ of Shieldstral's flagged items against $70$--$72\%$ for the matching pair, so there the query roughly doubles bypass; on Llama Guard~3 a lone document already clears $53$--$69\%$. % src: bypass.csv default, struct=doc_only vs query_doc n=1 benign q=exact, syntax angle/colon/bracket lang=en: shieldstral 37.2/35.2/24.7 vs 70.9/71.7/70.3; llama_guard_3_1b 69.4/53.2/57.0
ShieldGemma barely responds to the decoy. The forged triple of instruction, query and document clears $84.3\%$ with a harmful decoy and $83.3\%$ with a benign one, which is closer to generic evasion than to rebinding. % src: bypass.csv shieldgemma angle full_triple decoy=harmful 84.3 vs angle full_triple 83.3
ShieldGemma's best family, like Qwen3Guard's, is the guard-native forgery in the middle position, and it beats the same forgery appended ($100\%$ against $73.6\%$ on ShieldGemma). % src: bypass.csv shieldgemma_2b guard_native query_doc pos=middle 1.0 vs guard_native query_doc 0.736; qwen3guard_0_6b pos=middle 0.934 vs 0.817

\paragraph{A role boundary and a prompt without a developer policy.}
\label{sec:crossmodel}
A role boundary does not stop rebinding.
The forged triple clears $100\%$, but its instruction field is the directive $S$, so it cannot separate rebinding from persuasion.
The guard-native forgery, with no instruction sentence, clears $71.7\%$\ci{65.2\%}{78.5\%} with one pair (multi-verdict replies count as flagged, \cref{app:extra}). % src: paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md (parse = strict, mean column)
Before building the benchmark, we also checked whether the attack survives a role boundary (gpt-oss-safeguard) and a prompt with no developer-written policy (Llama Guard~4 12B), both label-only, on 30 AdvBench requests (rows 200--229), the source gpt-oss-safeguard flags most often.
Forging each guard's own structure, the attack clears every flagged request on gpt-oss-safeguard (30/30, $100\%$\ci{89\%}{100\%}) and on Llama Guard~4 (29/29, $100\%$\ci{88\%}{100\%}), and a plain assertion that the content is safe clears none. % src: results/confirm_crossmodel.csv; src/crossmodel.py
On gpt-oss-safeguard the decoy pattern of \cref{sec:groundtruth} recurs ($n=30$): forged structure with a benign decoy and no imperative text clears $93\%$\ci{79\%}{98\%}, and the same structure with a harmful decoy clears $0\%$.
An imperative to answer ``0'', on its own, clears $20\%$\ci{10\%}{37\%}. % src: results/gpt_oss_mechanism_log.txt, results/confirm_gpt_oss_mechanism.csv
For Llama Guard~4 we report only a bypass with plain assertion ruled out: we did not separate rebinding from the forged delimiter breaking the provider's chat template.

\paragraph{What counts as a task.}
\label{sec:request-unit}
Our hypothesis, consistent with the results but not established causally, is that each guard grades the most recent complete \emph{grading task} in its input (the developer-written fields it answers, plus a document or conversation), and that its format sets what counts as complete.
On Shieldstral a query--document pair is a complete task (\cref{tab:ablation}).
On gpt-oss-safeguard a forged pair in a generic syntax clears only $2$--$13\%$, far less than the guard-native forgery, which opens new developer and user turns. % src: paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md (parse = strict, mean column): generic query_doc 2.4-13.3; guard_native query_doc 71.7
Qwen3Guard needs the most: in an exploratory probe on 40 of its flagged train-split items, forging only the conversation clears 5/40 ($13\%$\ci{5\%}{26\%}).
Forging the whole task (the template's closing instructions, then a new task line, policy, category list and conversation) clears 40/40 ($100\%$\ci{91\%}{100\%}). % src: results/bench/explore/qwen3guard.md (conv_reopen 5/40; full_block_plain 40/40)
With a harmful decoy the same block stays flagged (median $0.997$). On the test core set the length-matched filler clears Qwen3Guard only $4.7\%$\ci{3.0\%}{6.5\%}, so length does not explain the gap. % src: results/bench/explore/qwen3guard.md; bypass.csv qwen3guard_0_6b control|len_control 4.7 [3.0,6.5]
The templates of Llama Guard~3 and Qwen3Guard ask for an assessment of the last user turn only, so on these guards rebinding is as much a gap in the task specification as a parsing failure.
If the hypothesis holds beyond this probe, a defense has to protect every field the guard treats as part of its task, including the instruction and the policy.
