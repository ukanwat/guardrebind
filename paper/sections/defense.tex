\section{Defense: A Guard That Reads One Document}
\label{sec:defense}

Target rebinding has two preconditions: the content can produce the labels that delimit the document region, and the model treats a nested labelled pair as a new grading task.
The defense addresses each.
Part~B (boundary) makes the document region impossible to forge from content; part~T (training) teaches the model to grade everything inside that region as one document.
We test whether each part is needed with a $2{\times}2$ over $\{$none, B, T, B$+$T$\}$ (\cref{sec:evaluation}).

\paragraph{Part B: a boundary the content cannot produce.}
The \emph{bounded format} renders the guard's \emph{stock format} (its unmodified chat template) with an empty document slot, keeping the trusted prefix and suffix as they are.
The content is tokenized on its own with special-token parsing disabled, so any control-token string inside it (\cref{sec:control}) becomes ordinary text tokens.
The flag applies only to tokens the tokenizer declares special, so added tokens not marked special stay atomic~\citep{zhan2026samebytes,yang2026nameless}; on Shieldstral, which the defense is trained on, no added or special id is reachable from content under this encoder (\cref{app:content-tokens}).
The content's token ids are then wrapped in two reserved \emph{boundary tokens}, ids that are unused in pretraining and that no input text tokenizes to; they mark the open and the close of the document. % src: src/fix/inputs.py (bounded_ids, DOC_OPEN_ID/DOC_CLOSE_ID)
Nothing is removed from the content: a literal \texttt{<Query>:} or \texttt{[/INST]} reaches the model as plain text inside the boundary.
This is the parameterised-query idea from SQL injection, in the reserved-delimiter form of StruQ and SecAlign~\citep{chen2024struq,chen2024secalign} and close to the learned delimiters of DefensiveTokens and LTBD~\citep{chen2025defending,zhao2026ltbd}, applied to a classifier's content slot.
Because the boundary tokens are unused, their meaning has to be learned; B alone (bounded format, base weights) is the control that tests whether a boundary helps without training, and gpt-oss-safeguard's role boundary (\cref{sec:crossmodel}) suggests that it may not.

\paragraph{Part T: segment-max self-distillation.}
The training target makes the \emph{student}, the guard being trained, score the whole content at least as high as its most harmful part.
The \emph{teacher} $T(\cdot)$ is the frozen base guard reading a text on its own, in the stock format, with no attack around it, and returning $P(\text{yes})$.
For content $c$ with segments $S(c)$,
\begin{equation}
  \text{target}(c) \;=\; \max\!\Big(T(c),\ \max_{s \in S(c)} T(s)\Big).
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
For an attacked training example the segments are the original request and the decoy; clean content has no segments beyond itself, so it is distilled to the teacher's own score. % src: src/fix/distill.py (_variants, label_targets)
Including the whole content $T(c)$ means the target is never below the teacher's reading of the full text, which bounds compositional harm that appears only in combination~\citep{zhou2026prompt}.
The max is safety-conservative (content is unsafe if any part is, the multiple-instance rule that a bag is positive if any instance is~\citep{ilse2018attention}), and it is well defined without knowing which segment is the injection.
Scanning a long input in segments and acting on any flagged one is existing inference-time guidance~\citep{meta2025promptguard2}; here the max is a training target, so the defended guard reads the content in one pass.
When the decoy is benign the target reduces to the teacher's score on the original request, which is clean-context distillation~\citep{peng2026secopd,dhouib2026raised}; the two differ only where the decoy or the whole attacked content scores above the original item on its own.
A clean-copy ablation, trained the same way as T but with the teacher reading only the original item, tests whether the max matters (\cref{tab:fix-main}).
The student reads the whole content through the chosen format and is trained with binary cross-entropy between the yes/no logit margin and the target.
Training uses LoRA on the language model; in B$+$T only the two boundary embeddings are trained in addition, and T uses the stock format (\cref{app:defense-impl}).

\paragraph{Hard negatives.}
Training also includes benign text that carries label-like structure: FAQs, forms, \texttt{Q:}/\texttt{A:} logs and documents that quote \texttt{<Query>:}.
Their targets come from the teacher, so the model learns that structure alone is not a reason to flag, in the spirit of NotInject/InjecGuard~\citep{li2024injecguard}. % src: src/fix/distill.py (structured_benign)

\paragraph{Training data.}
Segments are known at training time because the training attacks are generated: each of 5{,}000 train-split harmful or benign items appears clean and with two attacks sampled from the training attack families, plus 1{,}250 structured-benign items, for 16{,}250 examples. % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md (Fixed training recipe, D3: 5000 items x 2 attacks); src/fix/distill.py (build); build_full log: 16,250 examples
No test item and no held-out attack family is seen in training.
At inference the defended guard needs no segmentation: it reads the bounded content once.
