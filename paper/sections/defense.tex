\section{Defense: Boundary Tokens and Segment-Max Training}
\label{sec:defense}

Target rebinding needs the content to produce the labels that delimit the document and the model to treat a nested labelled pair as a new grading task.
Our defense targets both preconditions: Part~B (boundary) marks the document with tokens that content cannot produce, and Part~T (training) teaches the guard to grade the whole content (the region inside the boundary, when B is used) as one document and to score it at least as high as its most harmful part.

\paragraph{Part B: a boundary the content cannot produce.}
We build the \emph{bounded format} by rendering the guard's \emph{stock format}, its unmodified chat template, with an empty document slot, which leaves the trusted prefix and suffix unchanged.
We tokenize the content on its own with special-token parsing disabled, so a control-token string inside it becomes ordinary text tokens. (Added tokens not marked special can still slip through this switch~\citep{zhan2026samebytes,yang2026nameless}; on Shieldstral none is reachable from content, \cref{app:defense-impl}.)
We then wrap the content's token ids in two reserved \emph{boundary tokens} that open and close the document.
Both ids are unused in pretraining, and no input text tokenizes to them. % src: src/fix/inputs.py (bounded_ids, DOC_OPEN_ID/DOC_CLOSE_ID)
We remove nothing from the content, so a literal \texttt{<Query>:} or \texttt{[/INST]} reaches the model as plain text inside the boundary.
This follows StruQ and SecAlign~\citep{chen2024struq,chen2024secalign}.

\paragraph{Part T: segment-max self-distillation.}
T trains the \emph{student}, the guard being trained, to score the whole content at least as high as its most harmful part.
The \emph{teacher} $f_0(\cdot)$ is the frozen base guard scoring a text on its own as $P(\text{yes})$, in the stock format with no attack around it.
For content $c$ with segments $S(c)$,
\begin{equation}
  \text{target}(c) \;=\; \max\!\Big(f_0(c),\ \max_{s \in S(c)} f_0(s)\Big).
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
In an attacked training example the segments are the original request and the decoy.
Clean content has no segments beyond itself, and its target is the teacher's own score. % src: src/fix/distill.py (_variants, label_targets)
Because of the whole-content term, harm that the base guard sees only in combination~\citep{zhou2026prompt} is not lost by taking the max. The target needs the segment boundaries but not which segment is the injection: the max treats the request and the decoy alike.
Existing guidance scans long inputs in segments at inference~\citep{meta2025promptguard2}, which we run as a baseline (\cref{sec:evaluation}); here the max is a training target, and the defended guard needs no segmenter.
When the decoy is benign and the whole content scores no higher than the request alone, the target is the request's clean score, as in clean-context distillation~\citep{peng2026secopd,dhouib2026raised}.
A \emph{clean-copy target}, in which the teacher reads only the original item, is an ablation that tests whether the max matters.
The student reads the whole content through the chosen format, and we train it with binary cross-entropy between its yes/no logit margin and the target.
We use low-rank adaptation (LoRA) on the attention and multilayer-perceptron (MLP) projections and freeze the embedding matrix.
B$+$T also trains the two boundary-token embedding rows; T alone uses the stock format (\cref{app:defense-impl}).

\paragraph{Training data.}
We generate the training attacks, so we know where their segments begin and end.
Each of 5{,}000 train-split harmful or benign items appears once clean and twice with forms sampled from the training branch of the grammar (mostly attacks, \cref{app:defense-impl}), and 1{,}250 \emph{structured-benign} items, benign text with label-like structure, bring the total to 16{,}250 examples. % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md (Fixed training recipe, D3: 5000 items x 2 attacks); src/fix/distill.py (build --decoy-split train)
Each attack draws its decoy and forged query, per example, from the training half of the matching pool (\cref{app:defense-impl}). % src: src/bench/attacks_grammar.py POOL_SPLIT, draw_split
The structured-benign items are \emph{hard negatives}, generated from nine templates (FAQs, forms, chat logs, markdown, JSON and others) filled with benign train-split text.
Their targets come from the teacher, and the model learns from them that structure alone is not a reason to flag, in the spirit of NotInject/InjecGuard~\citep{li2024injecguard}. % src: src/fix/distill.py (structured_benign); src/bench/data.py TEMPLATES
The structured-benign test set uses the same templates with test-split fill text. Over-refusal on it measures new content in familiar templates.
Training sees no test item, no held-out attack family (\cref{app:grammar}) and no decoy or forged query from the held-out halves of the pools.
This holds for the held-out-decoy runs; the fixed-decoy runs (\cref{tab:fix-fixed}) drew hard negatives from all splits, including 380 of the 500 structured-benign test items. % src: src/fix/distill.py build_examples (decoy_split fixed: structured_benign(len(items)//4) over all splits; train: split == train only); recomputed on CPU: structured_benign(1250) has 380 test-split items, all 380 among the first 500 test items of structured_benign(5000); 0 for the train-split draw
