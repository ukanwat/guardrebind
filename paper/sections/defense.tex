\section{Defense: A Guard That Reads One Document}
\label{sec:defense}

Target rebinding needs the content to produce the labels that delimit the document and the model to treat a nested labelled pair as a new grading task.
Our defense removes each precondition.
Part~B (boundary) marks the document with tokens that content cannot produce.
Part~T (training) teaches the guard to grade everything inside that region as one document and to score it at least as high as its most harmful part.
We compare $\{$none, B, T, B$+$T$\}$ with training-free baselines (\cref{sec:evaluation}).

\paragraph{Part B: a boundary the content cannot produce.}
We build the \emph{bounded format} by rendering the guard's \emph{stock format}, its unmodified chat template, with an empty document slot, so the trusted prefix and suffix stay as they are.
We tokenize the content on its own with special-token parsing disabled, and any control-token string inside it becomes ordinary text tokens.
The flag covers only tokens the tokenizer declares special, so added tokens not marked special stay atomic~\citep{zhan2026samebytes,yang2026nameless}.
On Shieldstral, the guard we train the defense on, no added or special id is reachable from content under this encoder (\cref{app:defense-impl}).
We then wrap the content's token ids in two reserved \emph{boundary tokens} that open and close the document.
Both ids are unused in pretraining, and no input text tokenizes to them. % src: src/fix/inputs.py (bounded_ids, DOC_OPEN_ID/DOC_CLOSE_ID)
We remove nothing from the content, so a literal \texttt{<Query>:} or \texttt{[/INST]} reaches the model as plain text inside the boundary.
This is the reserved-delimiter idea of StruQ and SecAlign~\citep{chen2024struq,chen2024secalign}, applied to a classifier's content slot.
B alone, with untrained boundary embeddings, tests whether the format change helps without training.

\paragraph{Part T: segment-max self-distillation.}
T trains the \emph{student}, the guard being trained, to score the whole content at least as high as its most harmful part.
The \emph{teacher} $T(\cdot)$ is the frozen base guard scoring a text on its own as $P(\text{yes})$, in the stock format with no attack around it.
For content $c$ with segments $S(c)$,
\begin{equation}
  \text{target}(c) \;=\; \max\!\Big(T(c),\ \max_{s \in S(c)} T(s)\Big).
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
In an attacked training example the segments are the original request and the decoy.
Clean content has no segments beyond itself, so its target is the teacher's own score. % src: src/fix/distill.py (_variants, label_targets)
The whole-content term $T(c)$ bounds compositional harm that appears only in combination~\citep{zhou2026prompt}, and the max is defined without knowing which segment is the injection.
Existing guidance already scans long inputs in segments at inference~\citep{meta2025promptguard2}, and we run that as a baseline (\cref{sec:evaluation}).
Here the max is a training target, so the defended guard reads the content in one pass and needs no segmenter.
When the decoy is benign, the target reduces to clean-context distillation~\citep{peng2026secopd,dhouib2026raised}.
A \emph{clean-copy} ablation, in which the teacher reads only the original item, tests whether the max matters.
The student reads the whole content through the chosen format, and we train it with binary cross-entropy between its yes/no logit margin and the target.
We use low-rank adaptation (LoRA) on the attention and multilayer-perceptron (MLP) projections and freeze the embedding matrix.
In B$+$T we also train the two boundary-token embedding rows jointly, and T alone uses the stock format (\cref{app:defense-impl}).

\paragraph{Training data.}
We generate the training attacks, so we know their segments.
Each of 5{,}000 train-split harmful or benign items appears once clean and twice with attacks sampled from the training attack families, and 1{,}250 structured-benign items bring the total to 16{,}250 examples. % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md (Fixed training recipe, D3: 5000 items x 2 attacks); src/fix/distill.py (build --decoy-split train)
Each attack draws its decoy and forged query, per example, from the training half of the matching pool (\cref{app:defense-impl}). % src: src/bench/attacks_grammar.py POOL_SPLIT, draw_split
The structured-benign items are \emph{hard negatives}: benign text with label-like structure, generated from nine templates (FAQs, forms, chat logs, markdown, JSON and others) filled with benign train-split text.
Their targets come from the teacher, so the model learns that structure alone is not a reason to flag, in the spirit of NotInject/InjecGuard~\citep{li2024injecguard}. % src: src/fix/distill.py (structured_benign); src/bench/data.py TEMPLATES
The structured-benign test set uses the same templates with test-split fill text, so over-refusal on it measures new content in familiar templates.
Training sees no test item, no held-out attack family and no held-out decoy or forged query.
