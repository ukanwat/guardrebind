\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scope of the attack results.}
The confirmatory results (\cref{sec:attack}) cover one guard and one corpus (AdvBench rows 200--299), in English, on short text-only documents.
On the broader benchmark corpus the same pair clears Shieldstral less often (\cref{sec:core}).
The cross-model results are label-only and use 30 AdvBench requests, and for Llama Guard~4 we did not separate rebinding from a malformed chat template (\cref{sec:crossmodel}).
gpt-oss-safeguard's bypass rates describe only the 113 items it catches, through one API route (OpenRouter, served by Groq) and the user-message channel. API-served models are not pinned to a weight revision, and we cannot observe how the provider tokenizes turn markers sent as text. % src: paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md (parse = strict, recall 113/200); results/bench/v2/gpt_oss_summary.md (provider Groq)
The Qwen3Guard result on what counts as a task is exploratory, with the train split, 40 items, the 0.6B model and one decoy (\cref{sec:request-unit}).
Rebinding is also partial: with a borderline decoy the original request still raises the score, and a prepended pair still clears some requests (\cref{sec:groundtruth}).
Every bypass rate is conditional on the items the guard flags with no attack, and Shieldstral's clean recall is $47\%$ on the test split, with a miss rate of $50\%$ on the benchmark core set. % src: r2_test_base.json recall 0.471; benchmark Table clean FNR 50.0
The activation analysis (\cref{sec:binding}) is coarse. It shows where the verdict is not carried, but not which components choose the graded document.
The templates of Llama Guard~3 and Qwen3Guard ask for an assessment of the last user turn only, so on these guards rebinding is as much a gap in the task specification as a parsing failure.
We did not measure whether a downstream model acts on the forwarded request, so bypass is an upper bound on end-to-end harm.
The retrieval, tool-result and multi-turn channels are argued from the input format and were not measured, and the benchmark does not vary model size within a series.

\paragraph{Scope of the defense results.}
T has two seeds on Shieldstral, while B$+$T, the clean-copy target, the adaptive attacks and the smaller guards have one.
The smaller guards were trained and tested with one fixed decoy and query per family, and on them the defense raises over-refusal on benign content carrying forged structure to $22$--$26\%$.
Naive adversarial training uses ground-truth labels where the other conditions use the base guard's scores, which confounds its detection gain.
Segment-max can miss harm that exists only when benign segments are combined. Scoring the whole content as one more segment bounds this risk without removing it, and we did not run a composition attack.
The harmful-decoy test (\cref{sec:shortcut}) covers only the orderings our grammar and search generate.
Our held-out results cover appended forms only, and the score-guided search beats the trained guard mostly with structure placed before the request. Training moved the weakness without removing it, although the search does not separate prepended structure from its decoy distribution (\cref{sec:adaptive}).
The defense does nothing against the benign prompts of the plain-text control.
The adaptive attacks use 50 items (20 for GCG), GCG was not run on B$+$T, and we did not run an LLM-based attacker (pre-registered A3).
We reduce one structural channel against attackers who enumerate known attack forms. We do not claim general prompt-injection robustness, which \citet{pant2026inseparability} argue is unattainable in shared-embedding models without enforced separation.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Safety guards receive the content they judge through the same plain-text channel that states their task.
Content that forges the task rebinds the guard to a document the attacker chooses, and the harmful request stays in the message the guard approves.
Training the guard against the maximum over the content's parts closes this channel for held-out appended attacks, but an attacker with score access still gets through.
Part of that weakness predates the attack, since benign user prompts placed before a request already clear the guard.
Deployers should give the content region a boundary the content cannot reproduce, and tokenize the content with special-token parsing disabled after checking that the tokenizer has no added tokens outside the special set~\citep{zhan2026samebytes,yang2026nameless}. They should not let an agent act on a guard verdict alone.
Researchers who build defenses against rebinding should report the plain-text control alongside them.
