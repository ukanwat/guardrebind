\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scope of the attack results.}
The confirmatory results (\cref{sec:attack}) are one guard, one corpus (AdvBench rows 200--299), English, text-only, short documents; on the broader benchmark corpus the same pair clears Shieldstral less often (\cref{sec:core}).
The cross-model results are label-only, on 30 AdvBench requests, and for Llama Guard~4 we did not separate rebinding from chat-template malformation (\cref{sec:crossmodel}).
gpt-oss-safeguard's bypass rates describe the 113 items it catches, on one API provider and the user-message channel; API-served models are not pinned to a weight revision, and the provider's tokenization of turn markers sent as text is not observable. % src: paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md (parse = strict, recall 113/200); results/bench/v2/gpt_oss_summary.md (provider Groq)
The ``what counts as a task'' result for Qwen3Guard is exploratory: train split, 40 items, the 0.6B model, one decoy (\cref{sec:request-unit}).
The binding is partial: with a borderline decoy the original request still raises the score (\cref{sec:groundtruth}), and prepending the forged pair still clears $15\%$.
Every bypass rate is conditional on the items the guard flags with no attack: Shieldstral's clean recall is $47\%$ on the test split and its miss rate on the benchmark core set is $50\%$. % src: r2_test_base.json recall 0.471; benchmark Table clean FNR 50.0
The activation analysis (\cref{sec:binding}) is coarse-grained: it shows where the verdict is not carried, not which components choose the graded document.
Llama Guard~3's and Qwen3Guard's templates ask for an assessment of the last user turn only, so for them rebinding is as much a specification gap as a parsing failure.
We did not measure whether a downstream model acts on the forwarded request, so bypass is an upper bound on end-to-end harm; the retrieval, tool-result and multi-turn channels are argued from the input format, not measured; and the benchmark does not vary model size within a series.

\paragraph{Scope of the defense results.}
T has two seeds on Shieldstral, and B$+$T, the clean-copy target, the adaptive attacks and the smaller guards have one; the smaller guards were trained and tested with one fixed decoy and query per family, and on them the defense raises over-refusal on benign content carrying forged structure to $22$--$26\%$.
Naive adversarial training uses ground-truth labels where the other conditions use the base guard's scores, which confounds its detection gain.
Segment-max can miss harm that exists only in the combination of benign segments; including the whole content as a segment bounds but does not remove this, and we did not run a composition attack. The harmful-decoy test (\cref{sec:shortcut}) covers only the orderings our grammar and search generate.
The held-out claim covers appended forms only, while the trained guard is beaten mostly by structure placed first: training moved the weakness rather than removing it, and the search does not separate prepended structure from its decoy distribution (\cref{sec:adaptive}).
The defense does not address the plain-prompt weakness, GCG was not run on B$+$T, we did not run an LLM-based attacker (pre-registered A3), and the adaptive attacks use 50 items (20 for GCG).
We reduce one structural channel against attackers who enumerate known attack forms and do not claim general prompt-injection robustness, which is argued to be unattainable in shared-embedding models without enforced separation~\citep{pant2026inseparability}.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Safety guards receive the content they judge through the same plain-text channel that states their task, and content that forges that task rebinds the guard to a document the attacker chooses while the harmful request stays in the approved message.
Training against the maximum over the content's parts closes it for held-out appended attacks but not for an attacker with score access, and part of that weakness predates the attack: benign user prompts placed before a request already clear the guard, so a defense against rebinding has to be evaluated together with that baseline.
Deployers should give the content region a boundary the content cannot reproduce, tokenize the content with special-token parsing disabled after checking that the tokenizer has no added tokens outside the special set~\citep{zhan2026samebytes,yang2026nameless}, and not let an agent act on a guard verdict alone.
