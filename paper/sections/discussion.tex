\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The confirmatory study covers one guard, one English corpus and short text-only documents, and the pre-benchmark role-boundary and Llama Guard~4 checks are label-only on 30 requests (\cref{sec:crossmodel}). Bypass counts only items the guard flags without an attack, and Shieldstral misses about half of harmful items to begin with (clean recall $47\%$ on the test split). % src: r2_test_base.json recall 0.471
Bypass is an upper bound on end-to-end harm, and we did not test retrieval, tool-result or multi-turn channels.
Several results rest on small or single runs: the Qwen3Guard task probe (40 items), the adaptive attacks (50 items, 20 for GCG, none on B$+$T), and every trained condition except T, which has two seeds. The smaller guards and naive adversarial training had fixed decoys only.
On the defense side, our held-out results cover appended forms only, and the defense does nothing against searched benign prompts (\cref{sec:adaptive}). Segment-max keeps only the combined harm that the base guard itself detects, and we ran no composition attack. We do not claim general prompt-injection robustness~\citep{pant2026inseparability}.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Guards that read their task and the content as one plain-text stream can be made to grade a forged task, so a harmful request is approved unchanged. On Shieldstral and gpt-oss-safeguard the verdict follows the decoy, as rebinding predicts. Segment-max training nearly closes the enumerated appended channel with little change in clean detection, but an adaptive attacker with score access still wins, partly without rebinding. Deployers should train the guard, treat a content boundary as a cheap extra, and never let an agent act on a guard verdict alone. Defenses against rebinding should be tested against prepended structure and reported next to a plain-text control.
