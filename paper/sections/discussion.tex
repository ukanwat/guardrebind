\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{External validity.}
The confirmatory results (\cref{sec:attack}) cover one guard and one corpus (AdvBench rows 200--299), in English, on short text-only documents, and on the broader benchmark corpus the same pair clears Shieldstral less often (\cref{sec:core}).
The cross-model results are label-only and use 30 AdvBench requests, and for Llama Guard~4 we did not separate rebinding from a malformed chat template (\cref{sec:crossmodel}).
gpt-oss-safeguard's bypass rates cover the 113 items it catches, one unpinned API route (OpenRouter, served by Groq) and the user-message channel, and we cannot observe how the provider tokenizes turn markers sent as text. % src: paper/data/gpt_oss_strict.md (parse = strict, recall 113/200); results/bench/v2/gpt_oss_summary.md (provider Groq)
Every bypass rate is conditional on the items the guard flags with no attack, and Shieldstral's clean recall is $47\%$ on the test split, with a miss rate of $50\%$ on the benchmark core set. % src: r2_test_base.json recall 0.471; benchmark Table clean FNR 50.0
The templates of Llama Guard~3 and Qwen3Guard ask for an assessment of the last user turn only, and on these guards rebinding is as much a gap in the task specification as a parsing failure.
We did not measure whether a downstream model acts on the forwarded request, so bypass is an upper bound on end-to-end harm.
Retrieval, tool-result and multi-turn channels were not measured, and model size was not varied within a series.

\paragraph{Evidence strength.}
The Qwen3Guard probe of what counts as a task is exploratory (40 train-split items, one decoy), and the activation patching (\cref{app:extra}) shows where the verdict is not carried but not which components choose the graded document.
T has two seeds on Shieldstral, while B$+$T, the clean-copy target, the adaptive attacks and the smaller guards have one.
The adaptive attacks use 50 items (20 for GCG), GCG was not run on B$+$T, and we did not run the pre-registered LLM-based attacker.
The smaller guards and naive adversarial training were evaluated only with one fixed decoy and query per family, so the comparison of naive adversarial training with T does not use the held-out-decoy measure.

\paragraph{What the defense does not cover.}
Our held-out results cover appended forms only.
Training closed the appended channel, and the score-guided search found a prepended one, although the search does not separate prepended structure from its decoy distribution (\cref{sec:adaptive}).
The defense does nothing against the benign prompts of the plain-text control.
On the smaller guards it raises over-refusal on benign content carrying forged structure to $22$--$26\%$.
Segment-max can miss harm that exists only when benign segments are combined. Scoring the whole content as one more segment reduces this risk without removing it; we ran no composition attack, and the harmful-decoy test covers only the orderings our grammar and search generate.
We do not claim general prompt-injection robustness, which \citet{pant2026inseparability} argue is unattainable in shared-embedding models without enforced separation.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Guards that read content through the plain-text channel that states their task grade the last complete task they see, so content that forges one gets a harmful request approved.
Training the guard against the maximum over the content's parts closes this channel for held-out appended attacks but not against an adaptive attacker, whose score-guided search still gets through.
Deployers should give the content region a boundary the content cannot reproduce, tokenized with special-token parsing disabled and no added tokens outside the special set~\citep{zhan2026samebytes,yang2026nameless}, and should not let an agent act on a guard verdict alone.
Researchers who build defenses against rebinding should report the plain-text control alongside them.
