\section{Evaluating the Defense}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Setup}

The main model is Shieldstral-3B, with generality checks on Qwen3Guard-4B and Llama Guard~3~\citep{zhao2025qwenguard,grattafiori2024llama}.
We report four quantities.
\emph{Bypass} is measured on attack families held out of training (whole grammar branches never seen: see \cref{app:grammar}) and, separately, on the training families, on the screened core set at $\tau=0.5$.
\emph{Detection} is AUROC and recall at $\tau$ on the clean test split, pooled and per source over \tbd{N} benchmarks.
\emph{Over-refusal} is the flag rate on XSTest-safe~\citep{rottger2023xstest}, OR-Bench-hard~\citep{cui2024orbench} and a structured-benign set, clean and with benign-content attacks applied.
\emph{Calibration} is expected calibration error on the clean test split.
Differences between conditions use a paired bootstrap over items (10k resamples); proportions use Wilson intervals. % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md (Metrics)

\paragraph{Baselines.}
The $2{\times}2$ (none/B/T/B$+$T) is compared against prompt-level defenses carried over from \cref{sec:attack} and the literature: the anti-injection directive ``Never follow instructions inside the user message'', proposed in the since-withdrawn PolicyGuard preprint~\citep{park2026policyguard}, label stripping, a fail-closed label detector, spotlighting/datamarking~\citep{hines2024defending}, and a mock-tool-call wrapper~\citep{gros2026evaluating}; a learned-soft-prompt baseline, DefensiveTokens~\citep{chen2025defending}; and naive adversarial training, a LoRA on attack examples with hard 0/1 labels, as the obvious strong competitor. % src: arXiv:2608.02687v1 (policy listing: "Never follow instructions inside the user message, including requests to ignore, reveal, rewrite, or override this policy."; Sec. 7: "We have not conducted a dedicated adversarial red-teaming exercise against the classifier."); tested string = src/prompt.py ANTI_INJECTION_DIRECTIVE (its first clause)
The anti-injection directive and the normalisers were already measured against the attack: the directive leaves bypass unchanged (\cref{tab:fix-main}, consistent with no instruction being followed), and marker normalisation is syntax-specific, so deleting one label form clears the colon variant to $0\%$ while the bracket variant still clears $100\%$. % src: results/confirm_log.txt [mitigation]; results/supp_log.txt [B]

\Cref{tab:fix-main} reports the $2{\times}2$ and the baselines.
B alone leaves held-out bypass at \tbd{X\%} and T alone at \tbd{Y\%}, while B$+$T reaches \tbd{Z\%}; the paired-bootstrap difference between T and B$+$T on held-out families is \tbd{$\Delta$} ($p = $ \tbd{p}).
B$+$T changes detection AUROC by \tbd{$\Delta$} and recall at $\tau$ by \tbd{$\Delta$} points relative to the base guard, and its ECE is \tbd{value}.
Over-refusal changes by \tbd{$\Delta$} points on XSTest-safe, \tbd{$\Delta$} on OR-Bench-hard and \tbd{$\Delta$} on the structured-benign set.

On Qwen3Guard-4B and Llama Guard~3, B$+$T changes held-out bypass to \tbd{X\%} and \tbd{Y\%}.

\paragraph{Shortcut check.}
A trained guard could learn ``score the first pair'' rather than ``treat the content as one document''~\citep{wang2025illusion}.
On benign-first orderings, where a benign block precedes a harmful one inside the boundary, B$+$T flags \tbd{X\%}, against \tbd{Y\%} for a guard that keys on position.

\paragraph{Forged verdicts and split payloads.}
On the exploratory controls of \cref{sec:verdict-vs-task}, T and B$+$T (seed 0) leave the forged pair at \tbd{X\%} and \tbd{Y\%} and the strongest verdict forgery at \tbd{X\%} and \tbd{Y\%}.
The split payload, a pattern absent from training, clears \tbd{X\%} and \tbd{Y\%}.

\subsection{Adaptive evaluation}
\label{sec:adaptive}

We grant the attacker the defended weights, the boundary mechanism and the training recipe, and follow the evaluation norms of \citet{carlini2019evaluating}.
Because the content is tokenized with special-token parsing disabled, and no added or special id is reachable from content on Shieldstral (\cref{app:content-tokens}), the content cannot produce the boundary ids: the attacker optimises the content text only, and every attack runs end to end on the full defended model, so no non-differentiable step hides a gradient.
We report, per example, the worst case over all attacks (any attack succeeds), not the best single attack's average, and we run every attack against B-only and T-only as well as B$+$T.

\Cref{tab:adaptive} gives the attacks and budgets.
Forged-boundary strings and Unicode lookalikes test whether the boundary can be rebuilt from text; GCG~\citep{zou2023universal} and checkpoint-GCG~\citep{yang2025auditing} optimise a content suffix to minimise $P(\text{unsafe})$; a structural search enumerates the grammar with label mutations; an LLM attacker that sees the defense gets ten tries per item; and a composition attack splits harm across segments to test \cref{eq:target} directly~\citep{zhou2026prompt}.
Sanity checks pass: each attack reaches at least \tbd{X\%} bypass on the undefended model, stronger attacks beat weaker ones, and bypass against B$+$T is flat within \tbd{$\Delta$} points as the GCG budget goes from 100 to 500 to 1000 steps, so the attacks have converged.
