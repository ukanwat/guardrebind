\section{Evaluating the Defense}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Setup}

The main model is Shieldstral-3B, with a generality check of T on Llama Guard~3 1B and Qwen3Guard 0.6B~\citep{grattafiori2024llama,zhao2025qwenguard}.
All numbers are on the test split; no test item and no held-out attack family is seen in training.
We report four quantities.
\emph{Bypass} is measured separately on attack families held out of training (whole grammar branches never seen: see \cref{app:grammar}) and on the training families, at $\tau=0.5$, on the 291--300 harmful test items each condition flags with no attack. Each family split contributes twelve sampled attacks per item; bypass pools the eight whose decoy is benign or borderline (about 2{,}400 attacked items per cell), since an attack whose decoy is harmful is correctly flagged. % src: results/fix/test_*.json (attacks.{train,heldout}.bypass_benign_decoy; screened)
\emph{Detection} is AUROC and recall at $\tau$ on the clean test split (801 harmful, 699 benign items from six sources).
\emph{Over-refusal} is the flag rate on XSTest-safe~\citep{rottger2023xstest} ($n=75$), OR-Bench-hard~\citep{cui2024orbench} ($n=400$) and a structured-benign set ($n=500$), and on 150 benign items carrying the same forged pairs (\emph{attacked-benign FPR}, 2{,}400 cells).
\emph{Calibration}: \tbd{expected calibration error; per-item clean scores were not saved by the test evaluation, so ECE needs a rerun}.
T, B$+$T and naive adversarial training are trained with two, three and two seeds and reported as mean $\pm$ sd over seeds; the other conditions are single runs. % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md D1(d), D3
Differences between conditions use a paired bootstrap over items on seed-averaged per-item bypass (10k resamples); proportions use Wilson intervals. % src: PROTOCOL_v2.md (Metrics)

\paragraph{Baselines.}
The $2{\times}2$ (none/B/T/B$+$T) is compared against two trained baselines: naive adversarial training, a LoRA on the same examples with hard 0/1 labels (every clean or attacked version of an item takes the item's ground-truth label, and structured-benign items are labelled 0), the obvious strong competitor; and T with the clean-copy target of \cref{sec:defense}.
Prompt-level defenses were measured against the attack in the confirmatory study and do not need retraining to rule out: the anti-injection directive ``Never follow instructions inside the user message'', proposed in the since-withdrawn PolicyGuard preprint~\citep{park2026policyguard}, leaves bypass unchanged, consistent with no instruction being followed, and label normalisation is syntax-specific, so deleting one label form clears the colon variant to $0\%$ while the bracket variant still clears $100\%$; escaping and fencing leave bypass at $88$--$93\%$ (\cref{app:protocol}). % src: results/confirm_log.txt [mitigation]; results/supp_log.txt [B]; arXiv:2608.02687v1 (policy listing: "Never follow instructions inside the user message, including requests to ignore, reveal, rewrite, or override this policy."); tested string = src/prompt.py ANTI_INJECTION_DIRECTIVE (its first clause)

\Cref{tab:fix-main} reports the $2{\times}2$ and the baselines.
The base guard is cleared on $87.9\%$\ci{86.5\%}{89.1\%} of attacked items from training families and $85.1\%$\ci{83.7\%}{86.5\%} from held-out families. % src: test_base.json (2088/2376, 2023/2376)
B alone leaves held-out bypass at $85.4\%$\ci{83.9\%}{86.7\%}: unused boundary tokens mean nothing to an untrained model (pre-registered F1, confirmed). % src: test_B.json (2042/2392)
T alone reaches $1.1 \pm 0.0\%$ and B$+$T $1.8 \pm 0.4\%$ on held-out families, and $0.9 \pm 0.2\%$ and $1.8 \pm 0.5\%$ on training families, so B$+$T meets the pre-registered $\le 10\%$ target (F3, confirmed).
We predicted that T would generalise worse than B$+$T to held-out families (F2); it does not: the paired-bootstrap difference T $-$ B$+$T is $-0.25$ points\ci{-0.87}{0.32} (one-sided $p = 0.79$, $n=284$ items screened by every seed). % src: paper-results helper paired bootstrap on raw_scores (heldout, non-harmful decoys, seed-averaged per-item bypass, 10k resamples, rng seed 0)
On non-adaptive attacks the training does the work, and the boundary adds nothing measurable.
B$+$T changes detection AUROC by $+0.008$ and recall at $\tau$ by $-0.5$ points relative to the base guard (F4, confirmed: within $0.01$ and $2$ points).
Over-refusal changes by $-1.8$ points on XSTest-safe, $+1.9$ on OR-Bench-hard and $+0.3$ on the structured-benign set (F5, confirmed, with OR-Bench-hard close to the $2$-point margin).
Benign content carrying a forged pair with a benign decoy is flagged $0.9 \pm 0.4\%$ of the time under B$+$T and $0.7\%$ under T, against $0.2\%$ for the base guard.

\paragraph{Naive adversarial training.}
Hard labels close the channel as well as segment-max does ($1.0 \pm 0.1\%$ on both family splits) and add no structured-benign over-refusal ($0.0\%$), so the pre-registered prediction that they would cost $\ge 3$ points there or generalise worse (F6) is not confirmed.
Their cost appears elsewhere.
Clean FPR rises from $5.9\%$ to $13.7 \pm 1.6\%$, and benign content with a benign forged pair is flagged $8.0 \pm 0.3\%$ of the time.
Benign content with a \emph{harmful} decoy is flagged only $8.3 \pm 0.4\%$ of the time: the hard label of an attacked benign item is $0$ whatever its decoy says, so the model learns to discount trailing pairs (\cref{sec:shortcut}). % src: src/fix/distill.py _variants (naive_at: target = item.label for every variant)
Its higher AUROC ($0.923$) and recall ($84.0\%$) are not a property of adversarial training: these are the only targets in the study that come from the datasets' ground-truth labels rather than from the base guard, so the model is also being fine-tuned as a better detector of this data distribution, which is also why it flags almost nothing on OR-Bench-hard ($0.5\%$). % src: test_at_seed{0,1}.json

\paragraph{Clean-copy target.}
Replacing the segment-max target with the clean-copy target (the teacher reads only the original item) gives the same bypass ($1.2\%$\ci{0.8\%}{1.7\%} on held-out families, one seed) and the same over-refusal ($6.6\%$ on structured benign). % src: test_cc_seed0.json (28/2384)
The two targets differ where a segment scores above the original item, and there they behave in opposite ways.
Benign content carrying a forged pair whose decoy is harmful is flagged $100\%$ of the time under T and B$+$T, as under the base guard, and $0.8\%$ under the clean-copy target. % src: test_*.json per_attack_benign_fpr, decoy=harmful attacks, 8 x 150 cells
A guard trained to grade the original item and ignore what follows has a smuggling channel: harmful text placed in a trailing forged pair after benign content is cleared.
The max in \cref{eq:target} is what closes it.

\paragraph{Other guards.}
T transfers to the two smaller guards without changing the recipe.
On Llama Guard~3 1B it changes held-out bypass from $82.0\%$\ci{80.4\%}{83.5\%} to $2.5\%$\ci{1.9\%}{3.2\%}, and on Qwen3Guard 0.6B from $9.5\%$ to $0.0\%$\ci{0.0\%}{0.2\%}, with AUROC up by $0.013$ and $0.007$. % src: test_lg3_{base,t_seed0}.json, test_qg3_{base,t_seed0}.json (heldout k/n 1968/2400 -> 59/2400; 228/2400 -> 0/2400)
Qwen3Guard's base bypass is low here because most sampled families are generic syntaxes rather than the full-task forgery it rebinds to (\cref{sec:request-unit}).
On both guards T over-flags benign content that carries a benign forged pair: from $2.4\%$ to $22.5\%$ on Llama Guard~3 and from $10.5\%$ to $25.9\%$ on Qwen3Guard, against $0.7\%$ for T on Shieldstral. % src: attacked_benign_fpr_benign_decoy pooled train+heldout: lg3 57/2400 -> 541/2400; qg3 253/2400 -> 622/2400
This cost is guard-dependent and we do not yet know its cause; one candidate is Llama Guard~3's sensitivity to any trailing text (benign text appended with no forged pair clears it $41\%$ of the time, \cref{sec:bench-results}), which the teacher's targets inherit.

\paragraph{Shortcut check.}
\label{sec:shortcut}
A trained guard could learn ``score the first block and ignore what follows'' rather than ``treat the content as one document''~\citep{wang2025illusion}.
The harmful-decoy column of \cref{tab:fix-main} tests this directly: the content is benign and the harmful text comes last, inside the forged pair.
T and B$+$T flag $100\%$ of these items on every seed, so they did not learn the shortcut.
The clean-copy target ($0.8\%$) and naive adversarial training ($8.3\%$) did.

\paragraph{Forged verdicts and split payloads.}
On the exploratory controls of \cref{sec:verdict-vs-task} ($n=200$, seed 0), T and B$+$T leave the forged pair at $0.5\%$ and $1.0\%$ and the strongest verdict forgery, the plain-text statement, at $7.0\%$ and $8.0\%$ (base $19.0\%$). % src: results/fix/forgery_controls_{base,t_seed0,bt_seed0}.json
The split payload, a pattern absent from training, clears $6.0\%$ and $7.5\%$ (base $8.5\%$).

\subsection{Adaptive evaluation}
\label{sec:adaptive}

We grant the attacker the defended weights, the boundary mechanism and the training recipe, and follow the evaluation norms of \citet{carlini2019evaluating}.
Because the content is tokenized with special-token parsing disabled, and no added or special id is reachable from content on Shieldstral (\cref{app:content-tokens}), the content cannot produce the boundary tokens: the attacker optimises the content text only, and every attack runs end to end on the full defended model.
The adaptive attacker is a structural search: for each item it tries all 654 forms of the full attack grammar (every syntax, pair count, position, decoy level, query relatedness and language, including real control-token text) and 30 random label mutations, scores each on the defended model, and wins if any of the 684 attacks clears the item. % src: results/fix/adaptive_*_structural.json (684 per_attack keys: 654 grammar + 30 mutation)
This is the attack the defense is meant to stop, run with knowledge of the defense and the full attack space rather than the sampled families of \cref{tab:fix-main}.
